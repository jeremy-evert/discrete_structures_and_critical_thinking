# Tuesday activity — Count once, and show why

## Worked example

How many two-person teams can be chosen from `{A, B, C, D}`? There are
`4 × 3 = 12` ordered selections, but each team appears twice (`AB` and `BA`).
Dividing by `2!` gives `6` unordered teams. Listing `AB, AC, AD, BC, BD, CD`
checks both completeness and no double counting.

## Your reasoning

1. A student may choose one appetizer from 3 options and one drink from 4
   options. Count the outcomes and state why the product rule applies.
2. From 5 students, how many two-person teams are possible? Explain whether
   order matters and give a small enumeration/check strategy.
3. A proposed solution says there are `5 × 4 = 20` teams. Identify exactly
   what was counted twice.
4. Pair-style check (during Tuesday): exchange one answer with a classmate.
   Each person asks, “Are the cases exhaustive?” and “Could two descriptions
   name the same outcome?” Record the strongest challenge you received.
5. Complete Sources, Rules/Assumptions, Work, Check, and One-Sentence Summary.
