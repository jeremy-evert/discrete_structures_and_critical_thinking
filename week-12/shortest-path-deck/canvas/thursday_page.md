# Thursday, November 5: Show & Tell, defend a network model

Show & Tell is a real in-class activity: you make a claim public, defend it under questions, and give a peer one professional critique. It is **not a separate graded submission** (the old Show & Tell report was retired; see `assignments/show-and-tell-artifact.md`, kept as the reference for the four notes below). Your submission this week is the individual **Decision Gate**: one write-up you do on your own (Canvas shows the Decision Gate item and due date in this module).

## What to bring
One small graph situation of your own (or from Tuesday): a diagram, a table, or a few lines of code. Paper is fine. It must be non-sensitive; do not use real people or private data.

On your page write:
1. **Vertices**: what are the places?
2. **Edges and direction**: what does an edge mean? One-way or two-way?
3. **Weights**: what does the number mean, and in what unit?
4. **Objective**: what are you trying to find (a short path, a tour, whether things connect)?
5. **Your claim**: one sentence, for example "the best tour is 1-5-2-6-3-4-1 with length 34 under these rules."

## How a graph model is defended
Defend the **model** (is it the right graph?) and the **procedure** (are the steps right?) separately; a correct traversal on a bad graph is not a correct answer.
- Procedure: show every step is an edge in your graph and the total is added correctly.
- Model: say why the edges are what you say. What would change if an edge were two-way, or the weight meant something else?
- Say what your evidence does **not** show: for example, "this only holds if every edge is one-way".

## The session
| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0:00-0:10 | Label vertices, edge meaning and objective on a card |
| 0:10-0:18 | Critique an edge choice or a missing vertex |
| 0:18-0:48 | In small groups, each person takes a turn defending their graph and their path or traversal (a traversal is following edges through the graph) |
| 0:48-1:03 | Peer questions and critique of a model choice |
| 1:03-1:16 | Revise. Say whether the challenge changed the graph or only the procedure |
| 1:16-1:25 | Plan your Decision Gate |

Critique the claim, not the person. Narrowing your claim after a good challenge is a strength, not a failure. A wrong model you found and fixed during the defense is useful evidence of good reasoning.
