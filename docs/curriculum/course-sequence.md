# Discrete Structures and Critical Thinking: Course Sequence

This is a topic-first backbone inferred from the archived Canvas courses. It is a map, not a claim that every semester used every topic in exactly this order.

## Durable arc

1. **Orientation and learning practice** — welcome, belonging, attendance, degree awareness, and (in 2026) mindfulness, SMART goals, time-boxed work, and evidence-building.
2. **Logic, proofs, and sequences** — statements, reasoning, proof habits, sequences, and introductory matrices/functions.
3. **Algorithms and computation** — algorithmic thinking, implementation, correctness checks, and complexity/intuitive efficiency.
4. **Cryptography** — modular arithmetic and applied encoding/decoding.
5. **Recursion** — recursive definitions, recursive programs, and comparison with iteration.
6. **Counting and probability** — counting principles, combinations, permutations, inclusion/exclusion or advanced counting, and probability simulations.
7. **Relations** — properties and representations of relations.
8. **Graphs and trees** — graph models, paths/cycles, Hamiltonian ideas, trees, and applications.
9. **Boolean algebra and finite-state machines** — logical simplification, state models, and computation.
10. **Synthesis and reflection** — project/coding odyssey, career artifacts, and end-of-semester reflection.

The 2021–22 pattern grouped textbook work into six homework/exam cycles. The 2023–25 pattern used chapter-labeled weekly work, often with a second pass and pair-programming work. Spring 2026 is the clearest current sequence: Weeks 1–14 move from logic/proofs through functions and matrices, algorithms, cryptography, recursion, counting, probability, advanced counting, relations, graphs, trees, Boolean algebra, finite-state machines, and Farkle/Q-learning.

## Course rhythm that recurs or becomes explicit

- Problem work is submitted as a discussion or online artifact, with sources, assumptions, work, checking, and a concise explanation.
- Hands-on programming and demonstrations connect abstract concepts to observable output.
- Later versions alternate pair programming with show-and-tell/demo/reflection sessions.
- Career planning is a substantial strand: transcript/progress review, degree plan, resume, dream job, gap analysis, GitHub/LinkedIn, and learning/networking artifacts.

## Evidence notes

The topic skeletons in `lessons/` hold the teachable units. The reusable assessment types in `assignments/` and `quizzes/` deliberately preserve the archive's formats without copying semester-specific due dates or chapter numbers.
