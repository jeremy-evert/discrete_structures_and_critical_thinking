# Curriculum History Synthesis

## Scope and evidence

I reviewed the 12 Canvas snapshots in `archive/` (spring 2021 through spring 2026, including two fall-2022 course records with one empty snapshot) and skimmed the uploaded files. The snapshots are strongest as assignment/discussion metadata; only spring 2021 and spring 2026 retain substantial page/module material, and uploaded files are mostly images or a single project PDF rather than syllabi.

## What recurred

The mathematical backbone is stable even when labels change: logic/proof and sequences, algorithms, cryptography, recursion, counting, probability, relations, graphs/trees, Boolean algebra, and finite-state-machine/application work. Early courses use six recurring homework/exam cycles; 2023–25 use chapter-based weekly submissions; spring 2026 names the same backbone as a 14-week sequence. A repeated written-problem format makes sources, assumptions, work, verification, and summary visible.

Programming is not an add-on. In 2021, exams require small programs involving sequences, cashier's change, prime counting, Fibonacci performance, donut counting, and birthday probability. Later sections add pair programming, GitHub, a Git project, cryptography collaboration, finite-state machines, Hamilton paths/ant-colony optimization, and a coding-odyssey final.

Career and metacognitive work became a second durable strand after 2022: transcript/progress report, degree plan, resume, dream-job research, skill-gap analysis, professional networking/LinkedIn, and end-of-semester reflection. Spring 2026 adds a deliberate opening around mindfulness, SMART goals, Pomodoro/time blocking, STAR evidence, and 3-2-1 journaling.

## What evolved or appears experimental

The 2021–22 design is exam-heavy and textbook-section driven. The 2023–25 design expands the number of chapter submissions, adds repeated “round 2” work, pair programming, professional artifacts, and software-engineering feedback activities (especially spring 2023). Spring 2026 is a substantial redesign in calendar and language: weekly write-ups, an explicit pair/show-and-tell rotation, and Farkle/Q-learning as a late application.

The archive contains no Canvas quiz objects; “quiz” appears as assignment/discussion naming, including four software-engineering feedback quizzes in spring 2023. Quiz material should therefore remain a narrow reusable type, not be presented as a historically standardized quiz bank. Chapter numbers also vary (some omit chapters 9, 11, or 12; others split chapters or repeat them), so the topic-first organization is safer than chapter-first copying.

## Worth Jeremy's attention

- Confirm whether the spring-2026 sequence is the intended canonical backbone or an experiment; it is the most coherent topic map but is only one semester.
- Decide which career strand belongs in the permanent course outcomes versus a section-specific advising/career overlay.
- Establish a current grading/rubric policy: point values range widely, some work is zero-point or pass/fail, and the snapshots do not provide a stable syllabus.
- Preserve the five-part problem-solving rubric and pair/show-and-tell evidence requirements if those practices are still producing useful learning signals.
- Investigate archive completeness: most semesters have no pages/modules, and the “files” folders contain very few instructional documents, so absence from a snapshot should not be treated as evidence that a topic or syllabus never existed.
