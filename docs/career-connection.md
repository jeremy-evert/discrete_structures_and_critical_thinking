# DSCT career connection

**Status:** ACCEPTED course doctrine for Fall 2026 and future course builds unless explicitly revised.

## Purpose

Discrete Structures and Critical Thinking should help students notice that the reasoning they practice in this course has professional value. It should **not** become a second professional-development course hiding inside DSCT.

The career strand therefore stays deliberately light.

Architecture-level career preparation asks students to leave with a polished body of professional evidence. DSCT asks a smaller question:

> **Can you recognize what this course is teaching you to do, and can you point to evidence of that value?**

The existing **Career artifact sequence** remains one 5% course category, but its student experience should be two small connected moments rather than a semester-long resume/LinkedIn maintenance program.

## Moment 1: early-course professional connection

Early in the semester, students identify one role, problem domain, organization, community, or kind of work that interests them.

They make one bounded claim about why discrete structures and/or critical thinking might matter there.

Examples might include:

- logic helping distinguish valid conclusions from persuasive nonsense;
- graphs helping represent networks or relationships;
- counting and probability helping reason about uncertainty;
- sets and relations helping model categories and constraints;
- invariants helping reason about systems that change;
- evidence-checking helping evaluate AI-generated claims;
- formal models helping expose hidden assumptions.

This is a connection exercise, not a career-plan contract. Students are allowed to be uncertain and to change their minds later.

## Moment 2: late-course evidence revisit

Late in the semester, students revisit the earlier claim using evidence from their own work in the course.

They answer three small questions:

1. **What can I now do or explain that I could not do as well at the beginning?**
2. **What course evidence supports that claim?**
3. **Where might that ability matter outside this class?**

Possible evidence may include a Reasoning Odyssey Gate or checkpoint, a proof or counterexample, a graph/model, an analysis, a technical explanation, a Pair Reasoning artifact, a Show & Tell artifact, or another piece of work that genuinely demonstrates the student's reasoning.

The student is not required to turn that evidence into a resume bullet, LinkedIn update, portfolio page, or job-search package unless doing so is naturally useful to them.

## Relationship to the Reasoning Odyssey

The career connection may point to Reasoning Odyssey evidence, but it must not distort the Odyssey into career homework.

The Reasoning Odyssey remains the course's reasoning umbrella. Career reflection simply gives students two opportunities to notice that some of the thinking they are practicing has value beyond the classroom.

A student's personal World Bible or chosen lens may support the connection when it fits naturally. Do not manufacture a career connection merely to preserve continuity.

## Grading doctrine

The 5% career category rewards:

- a specific, plausible connection rather than generic claims that "critical thinking is important";
- evidence drawn from the student's real course work;
- honest revision when the student's original assumption changes;
- a short explanation of why the evidence matters.

It does **not** reward:

- visual polish;
- having a LinkedIn account;
- already knowing a career path;
- owning premium tools or hardware;
- producing a professional portfolio package beyond the scope of the course.

A student who says, in effect, "I thought this mattered for reason A; after doing the work I now think reason B is stronger" is demonstrating exactly the kind of critical thinking the course should reward.

## Scheduling boundary

The Fall 2026 course build should schedule one early-course moment and one late-course revisit inside the existing 5% Career artifact sequence.

The exact weeks may be selected by the course build so they fit the final Tuesday/Thursday rhythm and do not collide with major Reasoning Odyssey checkpoints or institutional calendar constraints.

Do not expand this strand into recurring weekly career homework without a new explicit course-policy decision.
