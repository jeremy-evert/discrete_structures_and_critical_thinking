THE PROBLEM: How do we write -5 in binary?
Sign-magnitude reserves a sign bit: 0101 is +5; 1101 is -5 (4 bits).
But 0000 is +0 and 1000 is -0. Two zeros! Addition also needs sign cases.
THE BIG IDEA: WHY TWO'S COMPLEMENT WORKS (8 bits)
An 8-bit register counts 0 through 255, then wraps to 0.
Think of a car odometer, or a clock. The wheel has no end.
One step BACK from zero is 11111111. That pattern behaves like -1.
Let M = 2^8 = 256. Adding M changes nothing after carry-out is dropped.
So -x and M-x land at the same place: arithmetic modulo M.
Flip x: (M-1)-x. Add 1: (M-1)-x+1 = M-x. That's the recipe!
For x=0, M wraps back to 0 too. Only one zero. Nice.

4-bit wheel (clockwise unsigned count; signed labels outside):

                         0:0000
              -1:1111              +1:0001
       -2:1110                            +2:0010
   -3:1101                                    +3:0011
 -4:1100                clockwise ->             +4:0100
   -5:1011                                    +5:0101
       -6:1010                            +6:0110
              -7:1001              +7:0111
                        -8:1000

Same circle, two readings. After +7 comes -8; after -1 comes 0.
CHECK: Python says 255, we got 255 (unsigned -1 pattern)

OVERFLOW LAB: range -128 through 127
Carry-out alone does not detect signed overflow. Compare carry INTO the sign bit with carry OUT.
For subtraction use a + ~b + 1, with the initial carry set to 1.
For ordinary addition, start the carry at 0. Different carries mean overflow.

Handling: flag the result before using it as a signed answer.
Use Python's exact integer answer, or widen BOTH operands and repeat.
Do not just attach extra zeros to a negative pattern: extend its sign bit.
