# Thursday activity — pair reasoning: search claims need an audit

Bring your Tuesday tree or use the supplied synthetic project/decision tree. A
**claim** is a sentence that says what the model can establish. A **model** is
the simplified graph or tree you are using. Work with a partner, but do not
divide the reasoning into separate halves: each person predicts, checks, and
records their own reasoning.

## 1. Before discussion — make your starting position

Write the claim you currently favor, the rule or evidence supporting it, and
one uncertainty. Examples include:

- “This traversal will find every named destination in the tree.”
- “This decision tree makes the supplied routing decision.”

Use only synthetic or supplied course examples. Do not add private information.

## 2. Challenge the model

Compare the original relationships with the displayed tree. Look for one
omitted edge, ambiguous split, duplicate case, or traversal assumption that
could change the claim. In this activity a **cross-link** is a relationship
between branches that is not represented by a parent-child edge.

## 3. Seek discriminating evidence

Do not settle a disagreement by voting. Use one concrete check:

- trace a case from root to leaf;
- search for a named vertex, including one that should be missing;
- compare the graph edge set with the tree edge set;
- add a missing question or edge on paper; or
- construct a **counterexample**, one case that defeats a broad claim.

Keep the Predict → Run → Explain loop: state the expected result, perform the
check, and explain which definition or relationship decided the result.

## 4. Revise or defend

Narrow or update the claim only as far as the evidence supports. A limited
claim is a strong result when the wider claim is not earned. State the remaining
limit and name the relationship the tree leaves out.

## 5. What to submit

Use the Thursday work to assemble your individual Week 13 **Decision Gate**:

- Sources;
- Rules / Assumptions;
- Work, including the labeled tree and two traces or searches;
- Check Your Answer, including one edge case and one omitted relation; and
- One-Sentence Summary.

Others in the course will see your work, and reading your classmates' work is part of the assignment. Use synthetic examples only and omit private information.

Also complete the individual **Pair Reasoning report** requested for this
activity. The report records reasoning under challenge; it is not a second copy
of the technical tree. Cover, compactly: your starting position, the strongest
challenge, the evidence that mattered, what changed or survived, your current
position, and what would change your mind next. Each partner's report must be
their own account.

The Decision Gate is the technical artifact. The Pair Reasoning report explains
what happened to your reasoning when another person tested it. Follow the
course site for the submission location and due time; do not infer either from
this package.
