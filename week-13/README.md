# Week 13 — Trees, Search & Decision Structures

**Tuesday, November 10 and Thursday, November 12, 2026**
**Question:** What information disappears when a network becomes a hierarchy?

This week turns a collection of relationships into a tree only when the tree
really preserves the question we need to answer. A tree is useful for search,
recursive traversal, and decisions; it can also hide a cross-link, a cycle, or
an alternative that changes the conclusion.

## Learning targets

By the end of the week, you can:

- distinguish a rooted tree from a general graph and identify root, parent,
  child, leaf, depth, and subtree;
- trace preorder, inorder (where applicable), postorder, and breadth-first
  traversal on a supplied tree;
- make and check a defensible search or decision-tree claim; and
- name one relationship a hierarchy omits and explain whether that omission
  matters for the claim.

## Prerequisites and materials

Bring your Week 12 graph vocabulary: vertex, edge, path, and the meaning you
assigned to an edge. You need paper or a local editable file. No outside text,
account, program, or AI tool is required.

## Tuesday: model, trace, and audit

Use [the Tuesday activity](student/tuesday-activity.md). The technical evidence
is a labeled tree, two traversal traces, and an audit of a relationship the
tree does not represent. The Tuesday AI Fluency check asks whether an AI-made
tree silently changes the original graph or decision rules.

## Thursday: Pair Reasoning and Decision Gate

Pairs use [the Thursday activity](student/thursday-pair-reasoning.md) to
challenge a search/decision-tree claim. Each student then submits two
individual objects:

1. the [Week 13 Decision Gate](student/decision-gate.md), using the five-part
   reasoning structure; and
2. the compact [Pair Reasoning report](../assignments/pair-reasoning-report.md).

The report records reasoning under challenge; it is not a duplicate of the
technical artifact. This is an ordinary Decision Gate week, not an Odyssey
checkpoint week.

## Revision and provenance

If feedback exposes a missing edge, unjustified split, or incorrect traversal,
revise the evidence and say what changed. The course retains the highest
recorded score for eligible revisions under the course grading model.
