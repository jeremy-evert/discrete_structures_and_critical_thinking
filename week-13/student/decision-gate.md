# Week 13 Decision Gate — Does the carry check out?

Submit one readable individual artifact. You may do it clearly on paper and
scan it, or type it. No particular software is required. You will also show it
in class on Thursday.

Choose your own binary addition: two binary numbers, each at least four bits
long, whose sum needs **at least two carries**. Do not use private student,
employee, health, or financial data. Make one claim: "my binary sum is
correct."

Use these headings:

1. **Sources** — write down your two binary numbers and say whether they were
   supplied or you made them up.
2. **Rules / Assumptions** — state the binary addition rules (including
   1 + 1 = 10) and the place values you use to convert between binary and
   decimal.
3. **Work** — show the addition vertically, one column at a time, with a place
   row, a carry row, and the answer. Then convert both numbers and your sum to
   decimal. Also show the full-adder truth-table row for one column of your
   addition that received a carry-in (give its A, B, carry-in, sum, and
   carry-out).
4. **Check Your Answer** — add the two decimal numbers and compare with your
   converted sum; then convert the decimal answer back to binary. Name one
   place an error could hide (for example, a missed carry), and say what this
   check does not prove.
5. **One-Sentence Summary** — state your conclusion and its limit.

This is the Week 13 ordinary Decision Gate in the 30% Decision Gate category.
It is not an Odyssey Checkpoint and does not add a second technical
submission. Use the course weekly rubric as the evaluation shape.
