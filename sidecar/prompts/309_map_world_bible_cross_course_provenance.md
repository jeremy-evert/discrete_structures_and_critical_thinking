# Prompt 309 — Map World Bible provenance across CS1, CS2, and DSCT

**Status:** FOREMAN-READY ARCHAEOLOGY CUT  
**Source quarry:** `sidecar/raw/2026-08-16_dsct_reasoning_course_design_dump.md`  
**Scope:** recover what the World Bible actually is in sibling/current source; do not decide the final DSCT carry-forward policy

## Why this is Foreman-ready

The design quarry has a strong direction: the **World Bible is worth preserving as a recognizable cross-course artifact family**.

Its purpose is personalization and continuity rather than mandatory fiction.

The quarry proposes a progression worth examining:

### CS1

Ownership and identity:

- what is my world?
- what exists in it?
- what did I build this week?
- what broke?
- what changed?
- what am I proud of?

### CS2

Design and engineering judgment:

- current state;
- design decisions;
- tests/evidence;
- known debt;
- changes and recovery;
- interfaces/contracts;
- what proposal was accepted or rejected and why.

### DSCT

Potential reasoning history:

- claims currently believed;
- definitions;
- assumptions;
- examples/counterexamples;
- sources;
- proof/test/simulation/model evidence;
- uncertainty/confidence;
- what changed the student's mind;
- what survived challenge;
- what stronger claim is not justified;
- what would change the student's mind next.

However, the quarry explicitly leaves open whether students literally carry the same Bible across courses or use a recognizable artifact family with optional import/carry-forward.

Recovering the real current/historical implementations is mechanical archaeology. Choosing the future policy is not.

## Work

1. Inspect current CS1, CS2, and DSCT repositories for student-facing and planning uses of `World Bible` and obvious predecessor/alias concepts.
2. For each course, document:
   - first introduction/onboarding point;
   - required/optional status;
   - fields/content requested;
   - update cadence;
   - relationship to weekly gates/checkpoints;
   - grading relationship if any;
   - whether the world is fictional, domain-based, or flexible;
   - examples offered;
   - how AI/tool evidence appears;
   - whether prior-course carry-forward is assumed, allowed, or absent.
3. Distinguish current doctrine from historical/provenance material.
4. Identify any existing portability/import language or bootstrap path for students without a prior World Bible.
5. Build a cross-course comparison table and provenance map in a DSCT sidecar report.
6. Highlight practices that already support the quarry's anti-bureaucracy principle: the World Bible should preserve meaningful context, not force ceremonial entries when nothing changed.

## Hard stops

Do **not**:

- decide literal carry-forward versus fresh course-native Bible;
- rename the artifact;
- decide required DSCT update frequency;
- choose which DSCT claims deserve longitudinal tracking;
- invent new grade weights/assignments;
- edit CS1 or CS2;
- change Savnac/Canvas.

## Acceptance

Complete when Jeremy + ChatGPT have enough provenance to decide the DSCT World Bible contract from actual course history rather than memory, with clear distinctions among what CS1, CS2, and DSCT already do.
