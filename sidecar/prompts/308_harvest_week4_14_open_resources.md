# Prompt 308 — Harvest Week 4–14 open/free resource candidates

**Status:** FOREMAN-READY RESEARCH / PROVENANCE CUT  
**Source quarry:** `sidecar/raw/2026-08-16_dsct_reasoning_course_design_dump.md`  
**Scope:** gather and classify source candidates; do not decide the final 11-week intellectual spine

## Why this is Foreman-ready

The resource philosophy is already decided:

If students genuinely need to buy a book, they buy one.

If they do not need to buy a book, do not replace one commercial textbook with one arbitrary free textbook merely to preserve the appearance of a textbook-driven course.

Instead, curate a catalog of the best parts of the best resources available and connect them with coherent course-owned instruction.

The external resources are ingredients. The course is the sauce that makes them belong together.

The quarry also identifies a useful source classification inherited from Architecture:

- **PRIMARY** — official/specification/normative truth layer where applicable;
- **ADAPT** — reusable/adaptable material with compatible licensing and attribution requirements;
- **LINK** — valuable public material that should be linked rather than copied/adapted;
- **TOOL** — official documentation/software/tooling;
- **CURRENT EVIDENCE** — time-sensitive evidence that must be date-stamped;
- candidate **EXPLANATION** — excellent pedagogical explanation that is not itself the primary truth source.

Known source families worth harvesting include, without assuming final selection:

- MIT OpenCourseWare Mathematics for Computer Science;
- Oscar Levin, *Discrete Mathematics: An Open Introduction*;
- Matthew Van Cleave, *Introduction to Logic and Critical Thinking*;
- Open Logic Project;
- SWOSU-owned materials;
- strong public university course notes;
- official tool/documentation sources;
- a small number of excellent public talks/videos/demos.

The mechanical job is to verify, classify, and map excellent sources. Choosing the final eleven-week sequence remains a Jeremy + ChatGPT decision.

## Work

1. Use the current DSCT topic map as a **coverage checklist**, not as a frozen week allocation.
2. For every current formal topic family, harvest strong current resource candidates.
3. Verify current availability, URL, source identity, license/reuse posture, and access requirements.
4. Prefer primary/open specifications and openly licensed academic/OER material where appropriate.
5. Distinguish free-to-read from legally adaptable.
6. Build source cards containing, where available:
   - technical concept;
   - Critical Thinking move;
   - best open reading;
   - primary/authoritative truth source;
   - alternate explanation;
   - short video/demo;
   - hands-on/computational activity;
   - coding/simulation bridge;
   - proof/counterexample bridge;
   - AI-verification angle;
   - licensing/reuse posture;
   - accessibility considerations;
   - why the candidate earned attention;
   - what specific slice appears useful;
   - what should **not** be assigned wholesale;
   - course-owned connective tissue likely required.
7. Preserve exact source provenance and retrieval date.
8. Report clear gaps where the course will probably need SWOSU-owned explanation/activity rather than pretending an external source solves the problem.

## Hard stops

Do **not**:

- decide which of the current twelve topic bundles must combine/move to fit eleven Week 4–14 slots;
- declare one textbook the course spine;
- assign readings to final dates;
- copy material whose license does not permit adaptation;
- turn historical ZyBooks material back into a required path;
- author the final weekly lessons;
- change grading or Savnac/Canvas.

## Acceptance

Complete when Jeremy + ChatGPT can perform the intellectual-spine decision with a verified source catalog in hand rather than vague memory or an unverified link pile.
