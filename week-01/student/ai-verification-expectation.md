# Love AI more. Trust AI less.

AI is useful in DSCT when it helps you explore: ask for an explanation,
another example, a question, a counterexample, a test case, or a different
approach. Treat every answer as a claim to inspect.

## Tiny example

Someone says: “A person who flips a fair coin twice must get one heads and one
tails, because the coin is fair.” It sounds reasonable. But “fair” describes
long-run chances; it does not force one particular two-flip outcome. `HH` and
`TT` are possible too.

Use the five-part method to challenge the claim:

- **Sources:** the meaning of “fair” and the possible two-flip outcomes.
- **Rules / Assumptions:** each flip has two outcomes; fairness does not fix a
  short run.
- **Work:** list `HH`, `HT`, `TH`, `TT`.
- **Check:** `HH` is a counterexample to “must get one of each.”
- **One-Sentence Summary:** Fairness does not guarantee one heads and one tails
  in two flips, so the claim is too strong.

The same check applies to AI output. Do not submit an AI answer as evidence
without inspecting and explaining it yourself. If you use AI later, label the
use and retain your own reasoning, tests, and conclusion as the evidence.
