## What this week is for

Discrete Structures and Critical Thinking is a reasoning course. We will use
precise language, definitions, discrete representations, small programs or
simulations, and tests to move from a question to a defensible conclusion.
This is a **Reasoning Odyssey**: the goal is not to memorize vocabulary or
produce fluent-looking answers, but to make the journey inspectable.

This week establishes the habits we will use later. No formal DSCT topic
reading is required yet, and Week 1 practice is low-stakes. Bring questions,
write down what you tried, and expect to revise your thinking.

## The reusable reasoning method

For a claim, problem, model, program, or AI answer, make the evidence visible:

1. **Sources** — What references, definitions, data, or examples are relevant?
2. **Rules / Assumptions** — What is known, what are you assuming, and what
   constraints apply?
3. **Work** — What steps, calculations, code, diagrams, or tests did you do?
4. **Check** — How did you test, cross-check, or challenge the conclusion?
5. **One-Sentence Summary** — What is the conclusion and method in one clear
   sentence?

The Check step matters: a confident answer is not automatically a supported
answer.

## AI expectation

**Love AI more. Trust AI less.** AI can generate explanations, examples,
questions, counterexamples, test cases, and alternative approaches. Fluent
output is not evidence. Inspect its definitions, assumptions, steps, sources,
tests, and conclusion. An unverified AI answer is not evidence.
