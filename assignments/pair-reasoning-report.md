## What Pair Reasoning is

Pair Reasoning exposes a student's reasoning to another human reasoner and
records what happened when that reasoning was challenged. The week's proof,
model, code, simulation, checker, graph, truth table, derivation,
counterexample, or other technical artifact is the week's normal
technical/Reasoning Odyssey evidence, produced and submitted through the
week's own technical channel. **This report is not a second copy of that
artifact.** Its evidence is the individual student's reasoning before,
during, and after another person's challenge — evidence that would not exist
without the pairing.

Programming may be the week's instrument, but it is never required. Pair
Reasoning must work the same way for a proof, a counterexample, a set/
function/relation model, a counting argument, a probability or simulation
claim, a graph or tree, a Boolean/SAT problem, a finite-state model, a
source/evidence analysis, or any other formal or computational reasoning
task the week assigns. There is no required driver/navigator role; partners
compare reasoning rather than divide labor.

## 1. Before discussion — individual starting position

Before substantive pair discussion, each student briefly records, on their
own:

- the claim, approach, prediction, interpretation, or answer they currently
  favor;
- why they currently believe it;
- an assumption, uncertainty, or confidence note, when useful.

Keep this compact — a few sentences, not a pre-lab essay.

## 2. During the pairing — one reasoner sharpens another

Partners compare reasoning, not just divide labor. Useful moves include:
comparing assumptions or definitions, locating where models or approaches
differ, naming the strongest part of the partner's reasoning, challenging an
inference/assumption/representation/evidence boundary, and seeking
discriminating evidence through proof, counterexample, trace, test,
simulation, source, definition, checker, or another method that fits the
week. When AI materially contributes, distinguish an AI proposal from
independently verified evidence.

Disagreement is not required. If partners already agree, the task becomes:
**test whether the agreement deserves confidence.** If partners disagree,
the task is not to win — it is to identify what evidence, definition,
assumption, proof step, observation, or counterexample actually separates
the two positions.

## 3. After the pairing — individual Pair Reasoning report

Each student submits one short, individual report. It must make the
student's own reasoning visible enough that one partner's report cannot
stand in for the other's. Cover, compactly:

1. **Starting position** — what I thought or proposed before the discussion.
2. **Strongest challenge** — the most important question, objection,
   counterexample, alternative, or assumption my partner raised.
3. **Evidence that mattered** — what proof, test, definition, trace, source,
   simulation, or similar actually bore on the disagreement or the
   confidence level.
4. **What changed or survived** — what I revised, narrowed, rejected,
   strengthened, or preserved after the challenge.
5. **Current position** — what I now believe and why.
6. **What would change my mind next** — the remaining evidence or question
   that could still move the conclusion.

A sentence or two per field is normally enough. Reference the week's
technical artifact by name or link if it helps the reader, but do not
re-explain or re-submit it here — this report exists only to make the
reasoning-under-challenge visible.

## Compact rubric

Score out of 4 points; no field-by-field bureaucratic breakdown is required.

| Dimension | What it rewards |
|---|---|
| Authentic engagement | The report shows real contact with a partner's challenge (or, if partners agreed, a real attempt to test that agreement) — not a fabricated disagreement or a rubber-stamped "we agreed." |
| Evidence over assertion | The response to the challenge points to a specific proof, test, definition, trace, source, or similar reason, not a bare restatement of the original claim. |
| Visible revision or preservation | The report shows what specifically changed, narrowed, strengthened, or survived — not just "I still think the same thing." |
| Individual accountability | The report is clearly this student's own reasoning trail; it could not be swapped with the partner's report unchanged. |

Do not reward disagreement for its own sake, and do not penalize a pair that
began in agreement and confirmed it through genuine testing.

## What this report is not

- Not a resubmission of the week's proof, model, code, simulation, checker,
  graph, truth table, derivation, or counterexample — that lives in the
  week's technical/Reasoning Odyssey evidence.
- Not a shared pair artifact — each partner submits their own report.
- Not a driver/navigator log — no role assignment is required or expected.
- Not required to involve programming.
