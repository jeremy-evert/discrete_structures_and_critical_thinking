# Evidence criteria

A strong receipt makes these visible:

1. **Precise claim** — `better` has an operational meaning rather than being a vibe.
2. **Assumptions/denominators** — the reader can see what was fixed and what was counted.
3. **Checked evidence** — more than one sample/interpretation is considered.
4. **Model limits** — the conclusion says what the experiment cannot establish.
5. **Revision judgment** — the student defends, revises, or refuses based on evidence.
6. **Reproducible trail** — sources, package/receipt, settings, and output are identifiable.

The numerical winner is not a grading criterion.
