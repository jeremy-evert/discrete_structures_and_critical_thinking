# Week 17 Final Individual Reflection

**Finals period: December 7–11, 2026**  
**Category:** Final individual reflection — 5%

## Purpose

This is a low-stress individual reflection on how your reasoning changed in
Discrete Structures and Critical Thinking. It is **not** a comprehensive
programming exam, a Reasoning Defense, a new project, or a request to repeat
every topic from memory.

Use evidence from work you actually did this semester: a Decision Gate,
Reasoning Odyssey checkpoint, Pair Reasoning or Show & Tell report, technical
demo/receipt, Week 16 Farkle evidence, feedback, revision, or another course
artifact. You may choose the evidence that most honestly shows your learning.

## What to submit

Write a readable individual reflection with brief references or links to at
least **two** course artifacts. Use headings or clearly labeled paragraphs for
the five prompts below. A compact, evidence-rich response is better than a
long chronology.

1. **Changed reasoning:** Identify one claim, method, or habit that changed
   for you. What did you initially think, and what do you think now?
2. **Evidence that mattered:** Describe one observation, counterexample,
   test, definition, critique, or calculation that changed your mind. Explain
   why it deserved to change your mind.
3. **AI judgment:** Describe one way AI helped your work *and* one way an AI
   output, confident explanation, or tool result needed checking. State the
   check you used or should have used.
4. **Transfer:** Name one discrete-reasoning method you expect to use beyond
   this class. Give a concrete context and explain the transfer without
   claiming it solves every problem.
5. **Uncertainty:** Name one question, limit, or skill that remains uncertain.
   State a reasonable next check, source, or practice step.

Close with one sentence in this shape:

> Because ______ evidence changed or supported my reasoning about ______, I
> now ______; I am still uncertain about ______.

## Evidence and authorship

- Cite or link the two artifacts enough that a reader can identify them.
- You may use notes, prior feedback, and AI as support, but the reflection
  must be your own account of your learning and judgment.
- If AI helped with wording or organization, name what it helped with and
  check that it did not invent your experience or evidence.
- Do not include sensitive personal details simply to make the reflection
  sound meaningful.

## What this does not require

You do not need to build new code, redo old assignments, produce a public
presentation, use a particular AI system, or prove that the course resolved
all uncertainty. Honest limits are evidence of good judgment.

## Submission timing

Use the course system's published final-reflection submission window. This
source intentionally does not invent a due date or platform-specific rule.
