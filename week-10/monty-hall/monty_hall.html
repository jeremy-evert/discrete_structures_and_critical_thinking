02 · Count the cases

Why switching wins 2/3

The reveal gives information because the host knows where the prize is and must show a goat.

Conditional probability means the chance of an event after we know that some other event has happened.

Your first pick has the car1/3Staying wins; switching loses.
Your first pick has a goat2/3The host removes the other goat. Switching reaches the car.

The host’s reveal does not make your first pick better. It concentrates the original 2/3 chance on the one other door that remains closed.

05 · Pause and predict

Check your intuition

Think through each one before opening its explanation. These are for practice, not a grade.

1. If your first pick is a goat, where is the car after the host opens a goat?

It is behind the one other closed door. A knowing host had to leave the car hidden, so switching wins in this case.

2. Does the host’s choice of goat tell us anything?

Yes. The host knows where the car is and must avoid it. The reveal is information shaped by that rule, so the two remaining doors are not equally likely.

3. What changes when the host opens a door without knowing?

Sometimes the host reveals the car. If we set those spoiled rounds aside, the first pick and the other retained door are each the car in half of the remaining rounds. The host’s knowledge changes which trials we get to observe.
