# Week 2 Evidence Portfolio rubric — 40 points

This rubric assesses recurring course work under the DSCT 50% bucket. It does
not create CS1/CS2-style semester category weights.

## Tuesday — Local AI readiness (20 points)

Use the shared `readiness_assignment_rubric.md` criteria and point values:

| Criterion | Full-credit evidence | Points |
|---|---|---:|
| Readiness and workflow evidence | Accurate status/code and, when READY, baseline, bounded request, diff, and final-test evidence. | 5 |
| Evidence interpretation | Distinguishes readiness, proposal, diff, test evidence, and correctness without overclaiming. | 4 |
| Component understanding | Correctly distinguishes Ollama, Aider, the model, and localhost. | 4 |
| Course-specific reasoning | DSCT reflection names claim, baseline/counterexample, diff change, and evidence limit. | 4 |
| Privacy and submission care | Correct filename and no avoidable identifying, credential, path, network, or unrelated-machine data. | 3 |

## Thursday — Container Connections (20 points)

| Criterion | Full-credit evidence | Points |
|---|---|---:|
| Layer and lifecycle reasoning | Preflight and lifecycle receipts distinguish backend, userland, image, container, process exit, removal, writable layer, and rootless identity. | 4 |
| Storage ownership and persistence | Named-volume and bind receipts identify type/source/target/RW state and prove the different persistence/lifecycle boundaries. | 4 |
| Exact evidence and repeatability | Receipts use full IDs, exact byte lengths, SHA-256, fixed image/input/transform/controls, and two distinct containers. | 4 |
| Portability and claim limits | Reflection cites the Bill/Brandy packet, identifies material differences, and makes only a bounded claim. | 4 |
| Safety and cleanup | Required receipts are searchable; stop conditions, Docker exclusion, and bounded cleanup are respected. | 4 |

Full credit requires an evidence-backed explanation, not merely successful
commands. A plausible idea missing its observable receipt needs revision;
contradictory or universal claims are incorrect.
