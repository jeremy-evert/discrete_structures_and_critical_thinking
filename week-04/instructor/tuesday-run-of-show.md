# Tuesday, September 8 — Logic, Claims & Proof

**75 minutes · 12:30–1:45 PM · Stafford 259**

| Time | Segment | Instructor move / visible output |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:08 | AI fluency | Put up a polished but invalid “examples prove correctness” argument. Students circle the conclusion and name what would make it follow. |
| 0:08–0:20 | Professional judgment | Ask students to separate a claim, a source, and an assumption in a consequential everyday or computing claim; collect one neutral example. |
| 0:20–0:35 | Technical lesson | Model proposition, implication, converse, predicate, and quantifier language. Emphasize domain and the difference between truth and validity. |
| 0:35–0:50 | Worked example | Trace the activity's three-test claim. Construct a rule that passes `0, 1, 2` and fails at `3`; verify that it is a genuine counterexample. |
| 0:50–1:05 | Individual work | Students complete `student/tuesday-activity.md`. Ask “Which universal word did your evidence fail to cover?” |
| 1:05–1:17 | Proof/counterexample mini-lesson | Show that one valid counterexample defeats a universal claim, while examples alone do not prove one. Model the five evidence headings. |
| 1:17–1:25 | Exit / bridge | Students write the one-sentence conclusion and retain it as Tuesday evidence. Preview Thursday's public defense and critique. |

**Materials:** `../README.md`, `../student/tuesday-activity.md`, and a board
or document camera. No external tool or account is required.
