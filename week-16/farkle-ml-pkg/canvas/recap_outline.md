# Week 16 Thursday: Recap Outline

## Tuesday — what we taught

- Fixed comparison before interpretation: target, strategy labels, rules, game count, balanced starts, and seed identity (Tuesday deck, “What is fixed before any game is counted?”).
- “Better” needs a criterion and decision rule chosen before repeated evidence, like choosing what a race measures before reading the stopwatch (Tuesday deck, “Why define ‘better’ first?”).
- Predict–run–explain makes a prediction visible and checkable before the initial sample (Tuesday deck, “Predict before the first sample”).
- Raw wins, games, starts, and denominators let a reader rebuild the initial win rates and keep the claim limited to that sample (Tuesday deck, “Initial sample: keep the raw counts visible”).
- One sample cannot prove a universal winner; Tuesday ends with a scoped sentence and a question to carry into Thursday (Tuesday deck, “Tuesday’s limited conclusion” and “Exit trace”).

## Thursday — what we taught

- The same comparison across seeds tests stability: compare each row’s ordering, minimum, maximum, and range instead of hiding variation in one average (Thursday page, “Inspect the same comparison”).
- The fallback bundle’s A−B differences (−40, +20, +60 percentage points) change sign, so the rows do not declare a winner (Thursday page, “Inspect the same comparison”).
- An alternate metric can change what a person prioritizes, but it does not silently replace the criterion or erase its denominator (Thursday page, “Ask whether another metric changes the story”).
- A model has limits: more games may add observations but cannot represent every rule, opponent, state, or whole-game claim (Thursday page, “Name what the model does not represent”).
- Peer critique and an AI-interpretation audit lead to a checkable final move: defend, revise, or refuse, recorded in the five-heading evidence receipt (Thursday page, “Critique a confident interpretation” and “What to submit”).
