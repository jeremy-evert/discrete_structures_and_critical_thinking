# Week 16 Tuesday: Farkle Evidence Glossary

## AI failure mode

A predictable way an AI-generated or polished explanation can mislead, such as hiding the denominator or ordering criterion and treating one sample as proof.

## AI

An artificial-intelligence tool or output. In this lab, AI output is another claim to audit, not the evidence itself.

## Algorithm

A step-by-step method for producing an answer or decision. This week you inspect results; you do not write a new Farkle or machine-learning algorithm.

## Assumption

Something treated as fixed or true for a comparison.

## Average score

Mean points per game, calculated by combining scores and dividing by the number of games.

## Balanced starts

Giving each strategy the same number of first-position and second-position starts. This addresses one fairness concern.

## Claim

A sentence that says what you think is true.

## Comparison

An examination of two or more choices under stated conditions.

## Criterion

The feature used to judge or order choices. A criterion answers what “better” means.

## Decision rule

A test declared before repeated evidence that says when to declare A, declare B, or refuse.

## Denominator

The bottom number in a division. For a win rate here, it is the number of games counted.

## Deterministic

Repeatable when the same inputs, including the seed, are used again.

## Evidence

Information that can be inspected and used to support or limit a claim.

## Farkle

A dice game in which a player may bank turn points or risk them by rolling again; a Farkle loses the unbanked turn points.

## Farkle rate

The number of Farkles divided by a stated count of turns or another stated denominator.

## Evaluation speed

The time needed to evaluate or run one game or comparison case.

## Fairness

Whether the comparison gives the choices a reasonable and stated opportunity to be compared, such as by balancing starts.

## Finite

Limited in amount. A finite sample contains only a particular number of observed games.

## Game engine

A program that implements game rules and mechanics. It is outside this week’s student task.

## Game count

The number of games included in a stated comparison or rate.

## Graded discussion artifact

The submitted work used for assessment and shared with classmates as a learning resource.

## Opponent setup

The stated player or strategy being played against and the conditions for that part of the comparison.

## Graded discussion

An assessed class discussion in which submitted work is visible to peers.

## Limitation

A boundary on what a source, model, or experiment can establish.

## Machine learning

A way of producing a rule from examples or experience. It does not make a finite result universal.

## Metric

A measurement used for a judgment, such as win rate or average score.

## Model

A simplified representation of a real situation. Here it represents the game, strategies, and comparison rules.

## Model limitation

A reason the model cannot answer a larger question, such as every possible opponent or rule set.

## Numerator

The top number in a division. For a win rate, it is the number of wins.

## Opponent

The player or strategy being played against in a comparison.

## Pseudo-random

Generated by a repeatable procedure that behaves like random choice for the experiment; a seed selects the repeatable sequence.

## Preparation effort

The work or time required before a strategy can be evaluated.

## Ordering criterion

The stated standard used to place one choice before another. It is the criterion behind a word such as “better.”

## Outcome

What happened in one game, such as an A win, B win, or tie.

## Overclaim

A statement stronger or broader than its evidence supports.

## Percentage point

The direct difference between two percentages. For example, 70% minus 30% is 40 percentage points.

## Peer-visible

Visible to classmates who are part of the course discussion.

## Prediction

A stated expectation made before checking the next evidence.

## Randomness

Uncertainty in which outcome occurs; different repeatable seeds can produce different samples.

## Raw count

The directly observed number of games, starts, wins, ties, or another event before it is turned into a rate.

## Raw counts

The directly observed numbers of games, starts, wins, ties, or another event before they are turned into rates.

## Range

The maximum value minus the minimum value in a set of values.

## Receipt

The evidence form that records Sources, Rules / Assumptions, Work, Check Your Answer, and One-Sentence Summary.

## Rule

A stated condition or action that is held fixed or used to make a decision.

## Seed bundle

A named group of seed samples used together for a repeated check.

## Simplicity

How few rules or components a strategy requires relative to another choice.

## Target score

The score needed to end the game.

## Threshold

The minimum difference or other amount that a decision rule requires before it declares a result.

## Sample

The particular games observed in one run.

## Scope

The exact conditions and cases to which a statement applies.

## Seed

A label that makes one pseudo-random sequence repeatable. Repeatable does not mean universal.

## Simulation

Repeatedly running a model to observe outcomes under stated rules.

## Strategy

A fixed rule for choosing an action from the information the strategy can see.

## Universal

Applying to every case in the larger category, rather than only to the tested cases.

## Variation

The way results can change from one sample or seed to another.

## Win rate

Wins divided by the games counted for that strategy.
