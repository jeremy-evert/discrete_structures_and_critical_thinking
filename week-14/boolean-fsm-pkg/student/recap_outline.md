# Week 14 recap outline

## Tuesday — five things we taught

- A Boolean variable holds `True` or `False`, and an assignment gives every
  variable a value.
- A truth table checks every small assignment, so one counterexample can break
  a claim about equivalence.
- A circuit is connected gates representing a Boolean expression; its rows can
  be checked against the written rule.
- A witness shows that an expression is true in at least one case, while a
  tautology is true in every case; one witness is not universal proof.
- A finite-state machine traces explicit state changes from named inputs and
  leaves its model limits visible.

## Thursday — five things we taught

- A defensible model begins with named states or variables, allowed inputs,
  rules, and assumptions.
- A complete truth table or two explicit traces turns a claim into evidence a
  peer can inspect.
- A challenge is useful when it tests a row, input, transition, or unstated
  assumption rather than attacking a person.
- A model limit names what the chosen representation cannot establish, such as
  authorization or an unlisted state.
- Revision is part of reasoning: the strongest final claim is the one the
  surviving evidence actually supports, with its boundary stated.
